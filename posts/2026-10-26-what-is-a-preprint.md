A **preprint** is a complete research paper that the authors post online **before** it has been peer reviewed by a journal. Anyone can read it for free, usually within a day or two of posting.

## How preprints work

1. The authors write their paper as usual.
2. They upload it to a **preprint server**, which does a quick basic check (is it real research, is it in scope?) but not full peer review.
3. The preprint gets a permanent link and often a DOI, so others can read and cite it.
4. Most authors also submit the paper to a journal. When it is published, the preprint is usually linked to the final version.

## The main preprint servers

| Server | Fields | Since |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [arXiv](https://arxiv.org) | Physics, mathematics, computer science, AI, statistics, economics | 1991 |
| [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org) | Biology and life sciences | 2013 |
| [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org) | Medicine and health sciences | 2019 |
| [SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com) | Social sciences, law, economics | 1994 |
| [ChemRxiv](https://chemrxiv.org) | Chemistry | 2017 |

Many countries and fields have their own servers too, and some journals now publish open peer-review reports alongside preprints.

## Why researchers post preprints

- **Speed:** results are public in days instead of the months a journal can take.
- **Priority:** the posting date shows who found something first.
- **Feedback:** other researchers can comment before the final version.
- **Free access:** anyone, anywhere, can read the work.

Preprints became widely known during the COVID-19 pandemic, when fast sharing of results mattered most.

## How to read a preprint carefully

Because preprints have not been peer reviewed:

- Treat the results as **preliminary** — they may change or be corrected later.
- Check whether a **published version** exists (the preprint page usually links to it).
- Look at the methods and sample size before trusting big claims.
- Be careful when sharing preprint findings on social media or in news stories; say clearly that the work is not yet peer reviewed.
