Thinking about a Master's or PhD in the United States? The first questions are always the same: **Which universities should I look at? How much is the application fee? How much is tuition? Do they take many international students?**

The answers are scattered across hundreds of university websites. So we put them on one map.

👉 **[Open the US universities map](../us-universities/)** — it is also on our [homepage](../), just under the world research map.

## What is on the map

The map shows **1,863 US universities that award master's degrees**. **764 of them also award PhDs.** Universities that do not award graduate degrees are left out, so every dot is a place where you can study for an MS or a PhD.

- **Dot colour** shows the research level: R1 (very high research), R2 (high research), other doctoral, master's universities and specialized schools.
- **Dot size** shows how many graduate students study there.

Together, these universities teach **558,462 international graduate students**.

## Tap a university — see everything in one place

Search a university or city, or tap any dot. You will see:

**For international graduate students**

- Graduate **application fee**
- Graduate **tuition per year** (for public universities, the out-of-state rate — what international students pay)
- **Estimated cost per year**: tuition plus room and board
- **International graduate students**: how many, and what share of all graduate students
- **Master's degrees and PhDs awarded per year**
- Student–faculty ratio
- **Top master's programs** and **top PhD fields**, with the number of degrees per year

**Undergraduate**: application fee, tuition, acceptance rate and whether TOEFL (or another English test) is required.

**Research**: papers, citations and h-index, with links to the university's [research profile](../universities/) and to **[find professors and supervisors](../scientists/)** there.

**Buttons** take you straight to the university's **Apply** page, website, financial aid page, cost calculator and its official government profile.

### An example: Georgia Tech

- Graduate application fee: **$95**
- Graduate tuition: **$30,646 a year** (out-of-state rate)
- Estimated cost with room and board: **$43,406 a year**
- International graduate students: **11,868** — **36%** of all graduate students
- **7,054 master's degrees** and **572 PhDs** a year; computer science is its biggest master's program

## Quick lists under the map

Below the map you will find lists that change with your filters:

- 💰 **Lowest graduate tuition** at research universities (R1 and R2) — some charge **under $10,000 a year**.
- 🆓 **No graduate application fee** — **549 universities** charge nothing to apply.
- 🌍 **Highest share of international students** — research universities where more than half of graduate students come from abroad.
- 🗺️ **States with the most international graduate students** — New York, California, Massachusetts, Texas and Illinois lead. Tap a state to zoom in.
- 📍 When you open a university, you also see **other universities within 100 miles**.

The typical (median) graduate application fee across all these universities is **$50**, and typical graduate tuition is about **$18,000 a year** — but it ranges from a few thousand dollars to more than $60,000, so it pays to compare.

## Filters

Narrow the map by **state**, by **type** (R1, R2, other doctoral, master's, or "offers PhDs"), by **public or private**, or show only universities where **at least 10% of graduate students are international**.

## Where the data comes from

All numbers come from **IPEDS**, the official database of the **US Department of Education** that every university receiving federal student aid must report to. Tuition and fees are for the **2023–24** academic year, the newest year with complete figures. Research numbers come from **OpenAlex**.

Fees and deadlines change every year, so always **confirm on the university's own website** before you apply — the Apply button takes you there in one tap.
