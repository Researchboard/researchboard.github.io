There are two ways to get a funded Master's or PhD abroad:

1. **Win a scholarship** — from a government or a university.
2. **Get hired by a professor** who has research money and needs a research assistant.

Our new **[Study Abroad](../study/)** section helps with both, in four tabs. Everything is free and links to the official sources.

## 🎓 Scholarships — with the official website on every card

The **Scholarships** tab lists fully funded and partial scholarships for international Master's and PhD students around the world — Chevening, DAAD, Erasmus Mundus, MEXT, Global Korea Scholarship, Chinese Government Scholarship, Fulbright, Swedish Institute, Stipendium Hungaricum, Türkiye Scholarships and more.

Each card shows:

- **What it covers** — tuition, monthly stipend, flights, insurance
- **Who can apply** and the **level** (Master's, PhD)
- **When to apply** — the deadline when it is confirmed, otherwise the usual timing
- An **Apply on official site** button and the **website address**

You can filter by country, level, **fully funded only**, **open or opening soon**, and **open to Bangladesh**. A **Closing soon** bar at the top shows what to prepare first — for example, the **Swedish Institute Scholarships for Global Professionals close on 15 January 2027**.

Every scholarship link is checked automatically each month.

## 💰 Funded professors — 10 countries where professors pay students

In many countries a Master's or PhD student is paid from a **professor's research grant**. This tab covers 10 of them: **Canada, Germany, South Korea, Japan, China, Australia, the Netherlands, Sweden, Hong Kong and Singapore**.

Pick a country and type your research topic. You will see:

- **How funding works there** — is a funded Master's common? a funded PhD? do you email a professor or apply to an advertised position?
- **How much research is funded** — the share of papers that name a funder
- **Researchers with funded papers on your topic**, at that country's universities
- The **top funders** and **top universities** for your topic

**Example:** in **South Korea**, for **solar cells**, **8,434 of 10,140 papers (83%)** were funded — and you can see exactly which professors wrote them.

## 🇺🇸 US active grants — professors who have money right now

A professor with an **active grant** has money to pay research assistants. This tab searches official US government grant databases:

- **NSF** (science and engineering) — searched **live**
- **NIH** (health and biology) — collected **weekly** for 40 popular health topics

Each result shows the professor, the university, the grant title, the **amount**, and **until when** the grant runs. For example, a search for **solar cells** finds **170 active NSF awards**.

Even before you search, the tab shows the **newest grants of the last 60 days** — new grants often mean new positions.

## 🇨🇦 Canada active grants

The same idea for Canada, from official open data:

- **NSERC** — science and engineering grants, refreshed weekly by topic
- **CIHR** — health research grants, searchable by any word

Grants are shown in Canadian dollars, with the professor, university, grant title and end date. In Canada, a professor's **NSERC Discovery grant** is the usual way thesis-based MSc and PhD students are paid.

## How to use it — a simple plan

1. **Pick a country** using the Funded professors tab — see if funded positions are common there.
2. **Shortlist universities** — for the US, use the [US universities map](../us-universities/).
3. **Find professors** working on your topic — with active grants (US and Canada tabs) or funded papers.
4. **Write to them.** Our guide **[How to find a research supervisor in 5 minutes](find-a-research-supervisor.html)** has an email template that gets replies.
5. **Apply for scholarships** in parallel — check the **Closing soon** bar every week.
