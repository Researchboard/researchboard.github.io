Three free tools cover most of what researchers and students need to find papers. Each is good at something different.

## At a glance

| | PubMed | Google Scholar | OpenAlex |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Run by** | US National Library of Medicine | Google | OurResearch (a non-profit) |
| **Covers** | Medicine, health and life sciences | Almost every field, plus books, theses and grey literature | All fields: papers, authors, institutions, journals, topics |
| **Best for** | Careful medical and biology searches | Quick searches and finding free PDFs | Data, statistics and analysis of research |
| **Advanced search** | Excellent (MeSH subject terms, filters) | Basic | Powerful filters and a free API |
| **Citation counts** | Limited | Yes | Yes |
| **Free data download** | Yes | No | Yes, fully open (CC0) |

## PubMed

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) is the standard database for medicine and the life sciences. Its records are checked and tagged with **MeSH** (Medical Subject Headings), a controlled vocabulary that makes searches precise: searching the MeSH term for heart attack also finds papers that say "myocardial infarction".

**Use it for:** clinical questions, systematic reviews, anything in health and biology. Tick "Free full text" to see papers you can read.

## Google Scholar

[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com) is the easiest place to start. It searches across fields and often finds free copies of papers on university sites ("All versions" link).

**Watch out:** its coverage includes non-peer-reviewed material, and you cannot see exactly which sources it indexes. It is great for finding, less good for systematic or reproducible searches.

## OpenAlex

[OpenAlex](https://openalex.org) is a free and fully open catalogue of the world's research, linking papers to their authors, institutions, countries, journals and topics. Anyone can download or query the data.

**Use it for:** counting and comparing research — by country, university or field — and building tools. **ResearchBoard is built on OpenAlex** (plus PubMed counts): every number on our [dashboard](../) comes from it.

## Which one should you use?

- **Writing a medical or biology paper?** Search PubMed first, then Google Scholar to catch anything missed.
- **Looking for a quick answer or a free PDF?** Google Scholar.
- **Comparing countries, universities or fields?** OpenAlex — or simply use ResearchBoard, which does it for you every day.
